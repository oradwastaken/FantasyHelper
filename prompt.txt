### Fantasy hockey

#### Categories
The 15 categories in configured in our league are separated into 2 types:
* Skater stats: G, A, +/-, PIM, PPP, SHP, SOG, FOW, HIT, BLK
* Goalie stats: W, GAA, SV%, SO

All of the skater stats are aggregated such that the team with the larger number wins.

For goalie stats, this is only true for W and SO. GAA and SV% are averaged over many games -- for GAA, the team with the lower value wins, and for SV%, the higher value wins. We also must take into account predicted goalie starts (GS), since some will have high averages but play very few games.

#### Stats
For the 2025-2026 season, we had an aggregate projections spreadsheet. See the "Player Values - Cats" sheet in the spreadsheet here:

The repo also has access to an API that can pull old stats from previous seasons. For the 2026-2027 season, it will be useful to have 2025-2026 season historical data. It will also be valuable to prorate some of these alues (for example, dividing the number of goals or assist by the number of games played, and then multiplying by 84 - the total number of games in a season). That way, players that were injured for part of last season won't be penalized for a future season

Finally all 15 up-to-date stats may be pulled live from the internet by calling the update_data() method.

#### Roster sizes
A complete team consists of 30 players.

Meanwhile, an "active" roster consists of 4 C, 4 LW, 4 RW, 6 D and 4 G per night. The active roster also includes 2 additional "util" slots, which can be any of C, LW, RW, D (so, any "skaters"). We can select different players from our roster to be considered "active" every night. We cannot exceed any number of slots, however. As an example, suppose we have 7 C playing on a given night, we could roster 4 of them under the "C" slot, 2 more as "util" players, but the 7th will have to be benched. There are so many slots available that this hardly acts as a limitation, as all of our best players will always have a spot open for them. However, it forces us to have a certain roster construction (for example, we can't have only LW, but we must draft some RW as well).

In addition to making trades, we are allowed to pick up players from Free Agency. Sometimes, a new player breaks out, and we can pick them up while dropping one of our worst players. More often, these are weaker players, but they are worth picking up if they have good schedules. Every team typically has a few slots (known as "streaming slots") reserved for dropping the weakest player and picking up a different (also weak) player with a better schedule. In our league, we are allowed a maximum of 5 of these "free-agency signings (known as "adds") per week, and only 43 throughout the season, INCLUDING the playoffs. These need to be portioned carefully throughout the season. A good team will always keep 5 per week for each week in the playoffs.

#### Schedule
Every week, we are matched up against a different opponent. By draft day, we will have access to the complete schedule, and will know who we play on what weeks. As this is a keeper league, we can predict who some of their players will be (and in particular, their best players). Therefore, we have some knowledge of which players which are strongest at which categories are most likely to play many games in a given week.

As an example, on week 1, we might play someone who has a a great goalscoring center who plays 4 games. On week 2, we might play against someone whose bruiser (i.e., a player who has lots hits) plays many games, but who won't have many goalscorers then. Therefore, we can optimize for a player with a good schedule who scores a lot of goals in week 1, and a different player with a good schedule who hits a lot in week 2.

The playoff schedule is also already determined, although we don't know who our opponent will be. We are fairly sure we will make the players, so we are ok with prioritizing the playoffs over the regular season. We need to maximize the number of games played by our best players during playoff weeks.

Finally, we have a rival in this league. This rival's team name is "McJesus Take The Wheel" although this is subject to change. (You may want to keep track of this rival based on the manager name or the keeper players.) We want to maximize our wins against our rival whenever we meet them this week.

Note: the priority should be to draft strong players. However, given 2 players of equal value for their given roster spot, having a good Playoff Schedule, Rival Schedule, and general schedule quality, should all be factored in as a tiebreaker. Note that it's easy for us to pick strong players; it's hard for us to distinguish a good schedule from a bad one. Also, if a player has an excellent schedule but isn't that great, we are likely to drop them in favour of a better one during the season, wasting a draft pick.
